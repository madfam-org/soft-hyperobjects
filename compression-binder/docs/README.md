# Compression chest binder

A pull-on chest binder in **breathable power-mesh**: a full-torso tube from the shoulders
to the ribcage that flattens the chest by **even, distributed** compression.

Part of the **Fashion Cabinet Commons** (FC-400, lane 9 — structured intimates). Official
visualizer and configurator: [Fashion Cabinet](https://fashioncabinet.app).

> A binder is the one intimates object where wrong compression is a safety question, not a
> fit preference. A cartridge that puts the two safety rules into the geometry — and caps
> the ease — is doing the most useful thing an open draft can do here.

## Why it earns its rank

Chest binding is done by many people, for gender affirmation and for sport, and it is done
unsafely far too often — with tape, bandages, or a too-small garment. All of those
**concentrate force on a narrow area**, which restricts breathing and can crack ribs. The
safe alternative is a purpose-made binder that spreads compression over a wide band of
breathable cloth. This cartridge encodes that safety directly into the draft:

**Rule 1 — compression is distributed, never concentrated.** The panel width is **floored**
(`min_panel_w`, default 200 mm) so the force is always spread across a wide band. The
negative ease is an explicit percentage, and it is **capped at 20 %** — a binder is not
shapewear, and more is not better. The finished chest ring is printed so the wearer can
confirm they are not over-binding.

**Rule 2 — it must not ride up, and it must breathe.** A binder that rolls up becomes the
narrow concentrated band rule 1 exists to prevent. So the panel is drafted **long** (past
the underbust to the ribcage), the hem is only **lightly** gripped (the lightest of all the
edges), the armholes are cut high for arm movement, and the back is a hair longer than the
front so it stays seated.

## Construction notes

Pieces: **front** (cut 1), **back** (cut 1, seated a touch longer). Two panels, joined at
the side seams and the shoulders — no gusset, no closure.

1. Join front to back at both side seams. The back is slightly longer; ease it onto the
   front so the binder seats over the ribcage.
2. Close the shoulder seams.
3. Bind the neckline and armholes flat with soft binding so no edge digs in.
4. Finish the hem with light elastic only — the lightest grip of all, so the binder does
   not roll up.

## Hardware

**None.** The binder is pulled on over the head; there is no closure to bridge, and this
lane does not invent one.

## Safety

This cartridge encodes the **distributed-compression** rule and **caps the ease**. It
cannot enforce safe **wear** — a binder is worn for a limited time, never slept in, and
never made from non-stretch cloth or improvised from tape or bandages. That part stays the
wearer's to keep, and the metadata says so plainly rather than burying it.
