# Dashiki

The loose pull-over tunic of West Africa — a wide, straight, unfitted garment worn over the
head, cut from a small number of **rectangular** panels and defined by the embroidered panel
around its V-neck and its short sleeves cut in one with the body.

Part of the **Fashion Cabinet Commons** (FC-400, lane 10 — heritage). Official visualizer and
configurator: [Fashion Cabinet](https://fashioncabinet.app).

> Two defining traits a Western tee-block draft erases: the rectangular cut-in-one construction,
> and the embroidered neck panel. This cartridge keeps the first and leaves the second — the
> meaningful surface — to its community.

## Provenance

The dashiki is widely worn across **West Africa and its diaspora**. This cartridge drafts an
everyday V-neck dashiki. It carries **no specific regional or ceremonial embroidery**, and the
embroidery ground is left blank for the wearer's own or a regional design — the geometry is the
garment's construction logic, not a copy of any particular tradition's motif.

## Why it earns its rank

**The body is rectangular and the sleeves are cut in one.** A dashiki is not a fitted shirt; it
is a wide panel folded at the shoulder with the sleeve continued out from the body as one piece
(a **T-shape**), in the wrapper-cloth economy that wastes almost no fabric. There is no set-in
sleeve and no side shaping — the fit is the **width**, chosen from a loose ease, and the drop of
the T is the sleeve. Drafting it as a fitted tee is the error this cartridge avoids.

**The placket panel is the embroidery ground — drawn, not invented.** The dashiki's defining
surface is the embroidered panel around the V-neck. This cartridge draws that panel as a
**facing** — the ground the embroidery sits on — sized to the neckline, and deliberately places
**no motif**. The embroidery belongs to the wearer, the maker, or the region; a commons
cartridge supplies the ground, not the design.

## Construction notes

Pieces: **front** (cut 1 on fold, V-neck, sleeves in one), **back** (cut 1 on fold, scoop neck,
sleeves in one), **facing** (embroidery ground, cut 1), **pocket** (breast pocket, optional).

1. Join front to back at the shoulder/sleeve-top seam (the top of the T) and the underarm/side
   seam.
2. Face the neckline with the embroidery-ground panel — embroider it first, in the wearer's or
   region's own design, before setting it.
3. Hem the body and the sleeve ends; set the optional breast pocket.

## Hardware

**None.** The dashiki is a pull-over with no fastening.

## Made to measure

Drafted to **chest** girth, **body** length, **sleeve reach** and the **neck opening**. The body
is a true rectangle and the fit is the loose width; every slider extreme renders watertight.
