# Battery Pocket Liner

A drop-in liner that gives a shell garment somewhere to carry a battery that is a
**brick**. Wave FC3-K (long-tail band, rank 296) of the FC-300 commons.

> **Nothing electrical is drafted here.** The bay window, the frame's flange
> footprint, the sew ring, the conduit run and the service loop are marked footprints
> and paths for a maker. No battery, cell, connector, BMS, or circuit is generated by
> this cartridge.

## What it is

| Piece | Cut | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `liner` | 1 | The liner panel: the framed bay window, the flange footprint, the solved sew ring, and the conduit run down to the side seam. |
| `bag` | 1 on fold | The pocket bag behind the window, cut to the flange width and to the **solved** depth. |
| `flap` | 2 | The closure flap, cut to the **measured** flange width so it closes over the rigid ring. |

## Why it earns a commons rank

The battery pocket is where heated and illuminated garments actually fail, and it is a
*pattern* problem that keeps being treated as an electronics one. The improvised
version is always the same: a patch pocket sewn to the lining, a 300-gram brick
hanging on two lines of stitching, the cable exiting through the same mouth a hand and
a zip use, and the connector taking the strain every time the wearer sits down.

Neighbours in this commons that look adjacent and are not:

- **`heated-glove-liner`** and **`illuminated-gilet`** are *carriers* for the
  conductive run itself — their problem is the conduit path, not the mass at the end
  of it.
- **`haptic-nav-belt`** carries many small components; each is light and each gets a
  strain relief. This one carries a single heavy rigid object, which is a different
  load problem entirely.

This piece exists because a rigid frame now exists to sew into
(`yantra4d/battery-pocket-frame`). It is also a **repair** as much as a garment: it
drops into a shell someone already owns.

## The seam that had to solve

**The window is cut to the frame's BAY, not to the battery.**

This is the mistake, and it is a one-line mistake. Given a battery, the obvious draft
cuts a window to it. But the frame is a rigid ring that is wider than its own bay by
`frame_w` on every side, and that extra width is *land* — it has to sit on cloth. Cut
the window to the battery and the flange has nothing to bear on, so the load goes
straight back onto the cut edge, which is what the frame was bought to prevent.

So the ordering is fixed in the kernel:

```python
BAY_W    = batt_w + bay_clear          # the frame's inner opening — the WINDOW
BAY_H    = batt_h + bay_clear
FLANGE_W = BAY_W + 2 * frame_w         # the frame's outer footprint — OUTSIDE it
FLANGE_H = BAY_H + 2 * frame_w
```

and every downstream piece reads the **flange**, never the battery:

| case | battery | bay (window) | flange | bag mouth | flap attach | match |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| default | 72 × 104 | 75 × 107 | 93 × 125 | 93.0 mm | 93.0 mm | ✅ |
| `brick_pack` | 120 × 170 | 123 × 173 | 155 × 205 | 155.0 mm | 155.0 mm | ✅ |
| extreme | 160 × 200 | 163 × 203 | 211 × 251 | 211.0 mm | 211.0 mm | ✅ |

Declared seams: `flap.attach ↔ bag.mouth` (both cut to the flange), and
`bag.seam_l ↔ bag.seam_r` (the bag's two sides, so a short bag cannot pass).

## The solved bag depth

The second thing that is solved rather than chosen:

```python
BAG_DEPTH = BAY_H + batt_t + cable_bend
```

Three terms, each earned. `BAY_H` because the battery has to sit fully inside the
frame. `batt_t` because the bag is folded, and a fold has to swallow a thickness — a
45 mm brick eats 45 mm of the bag's height that the flat draft does not show.
`cable_bend` because the cable coils **below** the battery; a bag cut to the bay alone
puts that coil at the mouth, and a coil at the mouth is the pinch that kills the cable.

| case | bay_h | batt_t | cable_bend | **bag depth** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| default | 107 | 22 | 30 | **159 mm** |
| `brick_pack` | 173 | 45 | 55 | **273 mm** |
| `thin_powerbank` | 143 | 10 | 20 | **173 mm** |

## Clamping, and the negative piece that verifies

Every derived dimension here is floored explicitly, and it is not decoration. Ask for
the smallest panel (180 × 240 mm) with the largest battery (160 × 200 mm), the widest
flange (24 mm) and the deepest flap (90 mm) and the naive arithmetic produces a
**negative** panel remainder. A piece drafted at a negative width is not rejected by
`verify()` — the kernel CCW-normalizes the outline, and what comes out is a piece with
a positive area, closed edges, and no complaint. It looks fine and cannot be cut.

So both panel dimensions carry a floor:

```python
liner_width  = max(liner_width,  FLANGE_W + 2 * MARGIN)
liner_height = max(liner_height, FLANGE_H + flap_depth + 2 * MARGIN)
```

At that extreme the floors fire and the panel is raised from the requested 180 × 240 mm
to **247 × 377 mm**, reported as `panel_floor_applied` so the raise is visible rather
than silent. The manifest also carries the two floors as `warning`-severity
constraints, so the studio says so before the render does.

The cartridge was probed at the **minimum and maximum of all 12 parameters**, plus
all-min, all-max, cross extremes, and every `target_piece` at both defaults and
all-max — 63 cases, all `errors=0` with every declared seam ok and no degenerate bbox.

## The sew ring is recomputed

`sew_pitch` is a *request*. The kernel divides the flange centreline's perimeter into
whole intervals and recomputes the pitch so the holes land exactly round the ring
rather than drifting off the last one (36 holes at a solved 12.111 mm at the defaults;
80 at exactly 9.0 mm on `brick_pack`). This is the same discipline
`magnetic-placket-shirt` uses for its magnet column, and for the same reason: a drifted
last interval is where a maker discovers the pattern was approximate.

## Hardware bridge

`notion.hardware_ref` → **`yantra4d/battery-pocket-frame`**.

The frame's `bay_w` × `bay_h` are fed `batt_w + bay_clear` and `batt_h + bay_clear` —
the same expressions the panel draws its window from — and `frame_w`, `sew_pitch` and
`bay_t` are shared outright. The remaining solid parameters (`frame_t`, `lip`,
`corner_r`, `hole_dia`) are derived from those, so there is one set of numbers, not two.

The frame declares `pocket_edge` as a `flange` cdg_interface driven by
`bay_w`/`bay_h`/`frame_w`/`sew_pitch`/`hole_dia`, and the garment's own `framed_bay`
interface is driven by `batt_w`/`batt_h`/`bay_clear`/`frame_w`/`sew_pitch` — so the
dimensional-handshake lane has a real coupling to check on both sides of the seam, not
just a name that resolves.

## Construction notes

- **Fabric.** `nylon-ripstop-shell` — liner weight, and its 110 °C iron ceiling
  governs anywhere near the conduit. Ripstop frays: the bag's seams are French or
  flat-felled, never raw.
- **Order.** Mark and cut the window, sew the frame in through all its holes while the
  panel is flat, then catch the bag under the flange, then the flap, then set the liner
  into the shell. The frame is unreachable once the bag is on.
- **The load path is frame → bag → seam.** The bag is caught *under* the flange, not
  topstitched to the panel face. Bar-tack the bag's mouth corners: that is where the
  battery's weight arrives.
- **The cable never uses the mouth.** It runs down the marked conduit to the side seam,
  where the shell garment's own seam allowance carries it, with the marked service loop
  giving enough slack that the battery can come out without the connector taking strain.
- **`conduit_exit` is a fraction of the measured side edge**, so the same value names
  the same height on the body whether the liner is 240 mm or 700 mm tall.

## Provenance

Original draft for Fashion Cabinet. Framed openings are old — a sewn-in rigid ring is
how a kiss-lock frame, a grommet, and a window in a camera bag all work, and none of
that is novel. The contribution is applying the frame-not-battery ordering where
e-textile pockets normally reach for a patch pocket, solving the bag depth from its
three real terms instead of picking a number, flooring the panel so the extreme case
cannot produce a piece that verifies but cannot be cut, and feeding the cable through a
seam rather than through the mouth a hand uses.
