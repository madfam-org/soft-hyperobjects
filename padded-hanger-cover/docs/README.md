# Padded Hanger Cover

The slip-on **padded cover** that turns a wire or thin wooden hanger into something a silk
blouse can hang on without shoulder dents. Two mirrored shell halves sew around a wadding
core, a gathered collar closes at the hook shaft, and a trim bow hides the join.

Part of the **Fashion Cabinet Commons** (FC-300, rank #257 — care & keeping).
**Pattern-only** — no hardware bridge. Official visualizer and configurator:
[Fashion Cabinet](https://fashioncabi.net).

## Why it earns its rank

Shoulder dents are the most common way a good knit is ruined by storage, and the usual
answer is buying new hangers. This covers the wire hangers already in the wardrobe, from
cotton offcuts and wadding scraps, and keeps a drawer's worth of them out of the waste
stream for the price of an afternoon.

## Pieces

`shell` (one half of the padded sleeve, cut 2 mirrored) + `collar` (gathered neck closing
around the hook shaft, cut 1) + `bow` (trim strip covering the neck gather, cut 1).

## The seam that solves

A hanger's shoulder is an **arc, not a bar**. The shell's inner and outer edges are drafted
as two **concentric arcs**, so the outer edge is genuinely longer than the inner by an
amount that depends on the sweep angle and the padded thickness — and that difference is
exactly what makes the cover cup the hanger instead of hanging slack. Both arcs are
polygonised and **measured**; the outer-to-inner difference is declared as the seam's ease
rather than absorbed silently, and a dart at the arc's crown takes up part of the excess
where the padding is thickest.

## Construction notes

Sew the two shells right-sides-together along the outer arc only, then turn and stuff — the
inner arc is closed last, by hand, over the wadding. The crown dart is stitched **before**
the shells are joined; leaving it until after buries it in bulk. Gather the collar onto the
hook shaft loosely enough to slide the cover off for washing, and use the bow to hide the
gathering thread rather than to hold anything.
