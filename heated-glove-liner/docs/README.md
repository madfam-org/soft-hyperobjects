# Heated Glove Liner

A four-piece knit glove liner drafted **around** its heater harness rather than with the
harness stuffed in afterward. Wave FC3-H (E-textile II) of the FC-300 commons.

## What it is

| Piece | Cut | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `trank` | 2, mirrored | Back-of-hand blank. Carries the serpentine heating traces across the metacarpals, a spur up each finger back, and the conduit run down the thumb-side seam. |
| `fourchette` | 2, mirrored | The finger side-wall strip. Cut to the *measured* slit run of the drafted trank. |
| `thumb` | 2, mirrored | Thumb gusset — a teardrop whose two curved sides close on each other. |
| `cuff` | 2, mirrored | Ribbed wrist cuff carrying the cable exit and the strain-relief plate footprint. |

## Why it earns a commons rank

Cold hands are a disability question long before they are a comfort question. Raynaud's
phenomenon, inflammatory arthritis, and cold-chain or outdoor work all get worse when
fingers stop moving, and the commercial answer is a sealed heated glove: when the element
fails, the glove is landfill. This cartridge inverts that. The liner is drafted to a
*measured* hand (five fit parameters, not four letter sizes), the heater is routed on
marked traces the maker follows with their own resistive yarn, and the bundle is held by
printed clips you can print again. Element failure is an element replacement.

It also fills a real gap in the commons: the existing e-textile cartridges
(`heated-vest`, `etextile-sensor-tee`, `led-costume-panel`) are all flat torso panels.
This is the first e-textile piece whose electronics have to survive **articulation** —
the hand is the hardest place on the body to route a wire, and the draft has to say
where the wire does *not* go.

## The seam that had to solve

A fourchette is the strip that forms the side-walls of the fingers. Its cut length is
the perimeter the three finger slits present — and the naive formula (`finger_len × 6`,
two walls per slit) is wrong twice: the slits for the index and little fingers cut deeper
than the middle two, and each slit ends in a semicircular tip arc rather than a point.

So the kernel does not use a formula. `_slit_path()` draws each slit — down one wall,
around a 12-segment tip arc, back up the other wall — and `SLIT_RUN` is the **measured**
sum of those drawn paths. The fourchette's `seam` edge is cut to exactly that number.
The trank's fingered top is then built as an alternating chain of named edges
(`finger_back_0`, `slit_0`, `finger_back_1`, `slit_1`, …) so the three slit edges can be
declared as one summed seam side against `fourchette.seam`:

```
fourchette.seam ↔ trank.slit_0 + trank.slit_1 + trank.slit_2
```

At the defaults that check reads 474.8 mm on both sides. It holds at every point in the
parameter range because both numbers come from the same drawn geometry.

## Hardware bridge

`notion.hardware_ref` → **`yantra4d/seam-conduit-clip`**.

The clip rides the thumb-side seam holding the heater bundle in a channel, so the wire
never crosses the knuckle flex crease — the single most common failure point in a heated
glove. The dimensional handshake is `conduit_tab`: it is the clip's sewn tab length
(`clip_len`), it drives the tab width (`tab_w`), and it is the width the trank's
`conduit-clip-*` drill footprints are marked to. One dimension, two commons, no drift.
`run_count` follows `heat_zones` so the clip carries as many channels as there are
circuits to route.

## Construction notes

- **Fabric.** A conductive-thread-compatible knit — the default fabric card is
  `jersey-algodon` (150 °C iron ceiling; check it before you press anything near the
  traces). `knit_stretch` cuts the cuff under the body measure so it grips.
- **Order.** Trace the heat routes and set the conduit clips on the flat trank *first*.
  Everything is accessible while the piece is a single layer, and nothing is.
- **Seams.** 6 mm throughout, overlocked. Glove seams are never pressed open; the
  allowance is deliberately narrow so the finished finger channel is not bulky inside.
- **The traces are marked, not drafted.** No element, yarn, or circuit is generated by
  this cartridge — the `trace`-kind internals are the *path* for a maker following it
  with their own resistive yarn and their own controller. Route power at low voltage.
- **Slit tips.** The semicircular tip is not decoration. A pointed slit puckers when the
  fourchette turns the corner; the arc gives the strip something to ease around.

## Provenance

Original draft for Fashion Cabinet. The trank/fourchette/thumb construction is the
classical glover's block — public-domain technique, redrawn here parametrically with the
e-textile routing as a first-class pattern feature.
