# Huipil (three-web blouse)

One of the oldest and most widely worn Indigenous garments of Mexico and Central America: a
rectangular tunic assembled from straight-woven webs. This is the classic **three-web**
blouse-length huipil of the Maya and other highland peoples.

Part of the **Fashion Cabinet Commons** (FC-400, lane 10 — heritage). Official visualizer and
configurator: [Fashion Cabinet](https://fashioncabinet.app).

> Where the FC-300 [huipil](../../huipil/docs/README.md) is a two-web tunic, this is the
> three-web blouse — a centre web carrying the neck flanked by two side webs, joined by the randa.

## Provenance

The huipil is Indigenous Mesoamerican dress. Its **woven designs** and the **randa** (the
decorative join-stitch) are specific to communities and weavers and carry identity and meaning.
This cartridge draws **no woven design** and **no randa pattern** — it supplies the web
dimensions and the neck, and the weave and the join-stitch are the weaver's.

## Why it earns its rank

**Three webs, joined by the randa — and the web width is real.** The garment is three loom widths
sewn side by side: a centre web and two side webs. The loom's usable `web_width` is a real
dimension, so the body width is `3 × web_width` — solved from the loom, not from a chest
measurement. The two vertical joins are the **randa**, marked here but drawn with no motif.

**The neck is the only cut, the body otherwise uncut.** The boat/round neck is cut into the
**centre web only**; everything else is straight webs used whole, folded at the shoulder, in the
loom-web economy. The sleeves are the web **overhang** past the body (a short cap), not set in.

## Construction notes

Pieces: **centre_web** (cut 1 on fold, with the neck), **side_web** (cut 2 on fold).

1. Weave the three webs to length in the weaver's own design.
2. Fold each at the shoulder; cut the boat neck into the centre web (the only cut).
3. Join the centre web to the two side webs with the **randa** join-stitch.
4. Close the side seams below the marked underarm, leaving the sleeve overhang open above; hem at
   the hip and bind the neck.

## Hardware

**None.** The huipil is a pull-over with no fastening.

## Made to measure

Drafted to the loom **web width**, **blouse length** and **neck opening**. The body width is three
webs and only the neck is cut; every slider extreme renders watertight.
