# Huipil

The **huipil** — one of the oldest and most widely worn Indigenous garments of Mexico
and Central America: a **rectangular tunic** assembled from straight-woven webs, joined
by straight seams, with a boat/keyhole neck. Its identity is rectangular construction and
the economy of the web; the artistry lives in the woven and embroidered bands.

Part of the **Fashion Cabinet Commons** (FC-200, Lane 4 — heritage). Official visualizer
and configurator: [Fashion Cabinet](https://fashioncabinet.app).

> **Heritage note.** This cartridge drafts the rectangular **garment geometry only**. The
> woven and embroidered designs that give each huipil its community identity are the
> maker's to supply and are **not** reproduced here. Offered with respect for the living
> traditions it comes from.

## Pieces

Two rectangular panels (front + back), each cut on the fold at centre, with a boat-neck
scoop, straight shoulder seams, and side seams left open above the arm-opening notch.

## Parameters

`chest_girth` (bound to the `chest_bust_girth` landmark), `huipil_length`, `neck_width`,
`neck_drop`, `web_ease`, `arm_opening`, and a marked `band_depth` for the maker's neck
band.

## Construction

The front and back panels are equal-width rectangles, so the straight shoulder and side
seams balance by construction (both verified to delta 0 by the seam-parity lane). Sides
are sewn only below the arm-opening notch; the decorative neck band is marked, not seamed.
