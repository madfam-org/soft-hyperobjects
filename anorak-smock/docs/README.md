# Pullover Anorak Smock

A ventile-cotton pullover anorak: no full-length zip, just a **short neck placket you pull over
the head**, a big kangaroo pocket, a two-panel hood, and a drawcord hem on a cord-lock.

Part of the **Fashion Cabinet Commons** (FC-400, rank #379 — outerwear).
**Yantra4D-bridged** (`cord-lock`). Official visualizer and configurator:
[Fashion Cabinet](https://fashioncabi.net).

## Why it earns its rank

The anorak is the original repairable rain layer — no zip to jam, no coating to flake, just
tight-woven cotton that swells shut in the rain and can be re-waxed for a lifetime. A pullover
cut on the fold has the fewest seams to fail. It deepens the thin `outerwear` family with a coat
built to be kept and mended.

## Pieces

`front` (cut 1 on fold, neck-placket slit) + `back` (cut 1 on fold) + `sleeve` (one-piece, cut 2
mirrored) + `hood` (one hood half, cut 2 mirrored) + `pocket` (kangaroo, cut 1).

## The seam that solves

A pullover front is cut on the fold, so its neck placket is a **slit, not a seam** — the slit
length is clamped under 28% of the length so it never runs past the chest. The kangaroo pocket
width is **derived from the front width** so a wide body gets a proportionate pocket, not a fixed
one that looks lost. The hood is cut to the measured neckline, the sleeve cap bisected against
the measured armholes, and the back neck width solved from the front's measured shoulder with the
flatten clamp.

## Construction notes

Flat-fell the side seams for a weatherproof pullover. Ventile beads rain by swelling its own
fibres — no coating to crack, so it breathes; re-wax rather than re-coat. Close the neck placket
with a short zip or two buttons.
