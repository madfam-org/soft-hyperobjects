# High-waist control brief

A high-waisted control brief in **power-mesh**: a deep tapered band above the navel, leg
**openings** (not tubes), a cotton-lined gusset, and a higher-covering back so it never
drags down at the seat.

Part of the **Fashion Cabinet Commons** (FC-400, lane 9 — structured intimates). Official
visualizer and configurator: [Fashion Cabinet](https://fashioncabi.net).

> Control is negative ease. A cartridge that prints the ease per zone as an auditable
> number replaces the size-letter-and-adjective lottery with something a wearer can
> reproduce and re-cut.

## Why it earns its rank

**Compression is a number, per zone.** Commercial control briefs sell the one thing that
matters — how much smaller than the body the garment is cut — as a size letter and a
marketing word ("firm", "sculpting"). Neither is reproducible. This cartridge makes the
negative ease explicit for each zone, and the zones differ because the body does:

| zone | default ease | why |
|:-----|-------------:|:----|
| waist | 20 % | the zone a high brief is bought for; a deep band holds without a seam that digs |
| seat | 12 % | less — over-compressing the seat just displaces volume to the thigh |
| leg opening | 4 % | the **gentlest**; a tight leg opening rolls into a cord that cuts in |

Each finished ring is printed in the metadata alongside the body measurement it came from,
so the control is auditable rather than asserted.

**The leg is an opening, not a tube.** Unlike the FC-300 shaping short this deepens, the
high brief has **no inseam**. The leg openings are the two lower corners of each flat
panel, finished with their own elastic ring sized off the thigh at the gentlest ease.

**The back covers more than the front.** Seat room is not added as an unbalanced side seam
— that would twist the garment. Both panels share **identical side seams**, and the only
front/back difference is the leg-opening scoop: the front scoops deeper (a higher-cut leg),
the back shallower (more seat coverage). That is what keeps the brief on the seat instead
of dragging down, and it leaves the side seams congruent by construction.

## Construction notes

Pieces: **front** (cut 1), **back** (cut 1, shallower leg scoop), **gusset** (cut 2 — self
+ cotton lining), **waistband** (cut 1, closes at a side seam).

1. Join front to back at both side seams.
2. Bridge the crotch with the gusset: its two ends sew to the front and back crotch flats
   (both exactly `gusset_w` wide). Enclose a cotton lining in the gusset — the hygienic
   standard for a brief worn against the skin.
3. Apply the leg-opening elastic at the gentlest ease so the openings lie flat.
4. Attach the deep control waistband to the assembled waist ring. Its upper edge is cut
   smaller than its lower edge so it tapers toward the true waist and does not roll down.
   Finish the top edge with soft plush-back elastic — the band's own negative ease is what
   holds a high brief up.

## Hardware

**None.** A pull-on control brief has no closure, and this lane does not invent one to
score a bridge. There is no `notion.hardware_ref` on this cartridge.

## Made to measure

Drafted to **waist**, **high-hip/seat** and **thigh** girths plus **body rise**. The panel
below the band keeps a real minimum height whatever the rise, and the leg scoop and
centre-front dip are clamped so the openings never invert at the extremes of the sliders.
