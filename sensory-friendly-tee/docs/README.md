# Sensory-friendly Seamless Tee

A tee drafted to **touch the skin as little as possible**: the body is a tube with the side
seams on the fold, the neck is a soft folded band, and there is no back label — the size prints
inside the hem.

Part of the **Fashion Cabinet Commons** (FC-400, rank #374 — adaptive).
**Pattern-only** — no hardware bridge. Official visualizer and configurator:
[Fashion Cabinet](https://fashioncabi.net).

## Why it earns its rank

For an autistic child or an adult with sensory processing sensitivity, a scratchy tag or a
raised side seam can make a shirt unwearable — and the market answer is a small, expensive
range of sensory-friendly basics. This puts the construction that makes a tee wearable into an
open pattern.

## Pieces

`body` (the tube, cut 1 on fold at each side) + `sleeve` (soft-set, cut 2 mirrored) + `band`
(the folded neckband, cut 1).

## The seam that solves

Cutting the body as a single tube means the neckline is a scoop out of one panel, and the
neckband must land flat — a **rippled neckband is exactly the edge a sensory-friendly tee
cannot have**. The band length is the **measured** sum of the front and back scoops, drafted
10% short so jersey stretch hugs it flat. The front scoop is clamped above the armhole base so
it never crosses it, whatever the proportions. The only seam allowed near the neck is the
band's own join, set at centre back away from the throat.

## Construction notes

Flatlock or coverstitch **every** seam so it lies flat with no raised overlock ridge. Choose a
smooth, cool fibre (OEKO-TEX modal) — a scratchy fabric defeats the purpose. Print the size in
the marked inside-hem ink box; never sew in a woven label.

## Cross-commons bridge

**None** — and deliberately so. A sensory-friendly tee carries no hardware: no snaps, no zips,
nothing hard against the skin. Pattern-only by design.
